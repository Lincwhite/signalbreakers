## Full Elevator Pitch (60-Second Version)

> **Signalbreakers: The Neon Protocol** is a cyberpunk puzzle-adventure game for Windows and Android. You play as LUMA-9, a young technician in a floating megacity who discovers that the city's communication network — the "Neon Protocol" — has been hijacked by an unknown entity.  
> 
> To uncover the truth, you must program robots, decode encrypted transmissions, and solve logic puzzles across eight themed districts: Token Town, Cipher Ridge, Datafall, and more. Each district teaches a different computational thinking concept — loops, conditionals, algorithms, parallelism — through hands-on puzzle gameplay rather than lectures or worksheets.  
> 
> Three difficulty modes (Explorer, Engineer, Blackout) make the game accessible to curious beginners aged 10+ while still challenging for experienced programmers. The story unfolds through environmental storytelling, character dialogue, and collectible lore fragments — no text walls, no hand-holding.  
> 
> Unlike most educational games that feel like coursework, Signalbreakers leads with narrative and atmosphere. Unlike hardcore programming puzzles that alienate newcomers, it offers generous hints and visual code representation. It sits at the intersection of Chants of Sennaar's language decoding, Zachtronics' programming depth, and Hacknet's cyberpunk mystery — but with a unique district progression system and ADHD-friendly design.  
> 
> One-time purchase on Steam ($14.99) and Google Play (free intro district + $9.99 unlock). No ads. No subscriptions. No loot boxes.

## Key Messaging Principles

1. **Lead with story, not education** — Parents buy "a game their kid will love"; teachers add it because it teaches. The primary hook is narrative + cyberpunk aesthetic; computational thinking is the secondary benefit.
2. **Avoid "worksheet" language** — Don't say "learn loops and conditionals." Say "program robots to solve puzzles." The educational content is implicit, not explicit.
3. **Three difficulty modes = three audiences in one game** — Highlight this on store pages; it's a rare feature that expands market reach.
4. **"No ads, no subscriptions" is a genuine differentiator** — Parents are tired of freemium mobile games with hidden costs. Lead with this on Android store page.
